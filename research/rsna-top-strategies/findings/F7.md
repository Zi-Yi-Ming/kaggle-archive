# F7: Labels vs multiplane EV

## Findings

### [1] High-placed competitors repeatedly name label quality — not multi-plane scale — as the remaining lever after ~0.93 LB; Raymond Yuen (81st) moved 0.938→0.949 on a single CoAtNet at 288×288 and attributes the gains entirely to labels.
- quote: "0.938 Lb. full train CoAtNet with 288*288. For now labels are still my greatest lever, especially pseudo labeling and blending different labels. … Update: Now 0.949. still label :D"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09 (thread; comment ~5 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [2] Archit Konde (45th) reports bigger models stopped helping and explicitly identifies labels as the bottleneck; a standard 2.5D CoAtNet at 224px then reached 0.950 single-fold after label work.
- quote: "The main thing was one check. On the 58 provided labels, my OOF predictions were already closer to the radiologists than the extracted labels the model was trained on. Bigger models weren't helping either, so I figured the labels were the bottleneck and started working on that. The model is pretty standard: 2.5D CoAtNet at 224"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09 (comment ~11 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [3] An 18th-place public writeup states the competitive thesis directly: the image model is the well-trodden 80% and report-label construction is the hard 20% that decides rank; every image-side lever tops out at the label ceiling.
- quote: "The image model is the well-trodden 80%. A clean 12-label target for 4,349 unlabeled studies is the hard 20% that actually decides where you land. … Deriving labels from that text is the actual competition, not a preprocessing step: every lever downstream (model architecture, augmentation, ensembling) tops out at whatever ceiling the label quality sets."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-08-09
- confidence: high

### [4] The same 18th-place pipeline quantifies the label ceiling: a carefully revised LLM report extractor reached 0.833 mean per-class accuracy / 0.766 mean F1 against the 58 expert gold studies, while a perfect report reader is estimated to cap around 0.88–0.92 because reports and images disagree.
- quote: "The revised prompt reached 0.833 mean accuracy, 0.766 mean F1 … gold labels here come from an expert re-reading the actual images, not from the text report, so even a perfect report reader has an intrinsic accuracy ceiling somewhere around 0.88 to 0.92 … that gap is probably the real limiting factor on this whole approach right now, ahead of anything on the image side."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-08-09
- confidence: high

### [5] Multi-source LLM label consensus is already a production pattern: the 18th-place team fused their extractor with a third-party public dataset (pilkwang/rsna-knee-llm-labels), weighting each source by its own per-class accuracy on the 58 gold studies and emitting soft labels on disagreement.
- quote: "`build_labels.py` supports fusing up to three sources: the primary extractor above, an optional second-family cross-check model … and a third … public Kaggle dataset from another competitor (pilkwang/rsna-knee-llm-labels) … Each source's hard vote is weighted by its own measured per-class accuracy against the 58 gold studies … When they disagree, the output is a soft label: a reliability-weighted average of every vote"
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-08-09
- confidence: high

### [6] Multi-plane fusion is used as default series selection but is not claimed as the scoring lever; Less (98th) reports a single-fold CoAtNet at 384×384 reaching 0.937 while multi-plane fusion was ineffective.
- quote: "Single fold, CoAtNet, 384*384, LB 0.937, but fusion is ineffective."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09 (comment ~14 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [7] Counterpoint from tennogh (9th): custom labels were not measurably better than public ones and resolution is not a big driver; 0.942 raw / 0.943 with TTA at 288×288 came from incremental improvements and OOF pseudo-labels correlating with LB.
- quote: "So far 0.942 raw, 0.943 with TTA at 288x288. OOF pseudo-labels have been pretty well correlated with LB. … I have my own labels but they are not measurably better than the public ones. I'm not sure resolution is such a big driver."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09 (comment ~24 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [8] Yann Majewski (76th) isolates a concrete label ROI number: combining his labels with a public dataset lifted LB by +0.015; pretraining + longer training + regularization then took a small ResNet at 224×224 to 0.936 — i.e. low-res single-plane scale is enough once labels are decent.
- quote: "I did a little bit of label work, combined my labels with some public dataset and it increased lb score by 0.015 … Then some pre training on the encoder and training longer with more regularization helped to reach 0.936!"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09 (comment ~25 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [9] A public backbone-history survey of the same competition concludes the problem is study-construction + supervision first and backbone ranking second, and that backbone swaps may underperform label/normalization/series-selection work.
- quote: "That is why I currently view this as a study-construction and supervision problem first, and a backbone-ranking problem second. … If report-derived supervision and DICOM normalization dominate the error budget, then changing the backbone may produce much smaller gains than improving labels, physical coordinates and series selection."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740164
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09-08
- confidence: medium

### [10] Even crude public LLM labels unlock mid-0.90 single models: Shiv Satyam reports public GPT-5.6 labels + a mundane ResNet34 linear head with no augmentations reaching 0.910 — implying label source quality is on the critical path from mid-pack to 0.91 before any multi-plane work.
- quote: "They are only working well if you have strong labels. The public `GPT 5.6` labelled data got me to .910 with a simple Resnet34 using a mundane linear head with no augmentations."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740164
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09-27 (comment ~2 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: medium

### [11] The public pilkwang multi-extractor LLM label set operationalizes multi-source consensus with explicit silence handling (UNK ≠ NO) across lexicon + local-LLM + hosted-LLM readers — the exact failure mode (unmentioned finding written as negative) that collapses soft-label training.
- quote: "Verdicts are YES / NO / UNK, where NO means the report denies the finding or calls the structure normal and UNK means the report does not mention it. … A finding the report never names is UNK, not NO. … `report_labeler` is a lexicon: fast, free, and strong on the vocabulary it knows. Its failure mode is silence"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/pilkwang/rsna-knee-llm-labels
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-08 (dataset updated ~2 months before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [12] Chris Deotte (86th) notes pseudo-labels erase the "single model" distinction: any single model trained on pseudo-labels can match ensemble CV, so the real quality signal is label provenance (report-extracted vs pseudo) rather than model count or multi-plane stack size.
- quote: "There are two types of single models. Those using original report extracted labels and those using pseudo labels. If we use pseudo labels, then every single model can match our best ensemble CV score and reporting that hides the fact that it isn't actually 'single model'."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09 (comment ~2 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [13] Transferable medical-imaging evidence: a 2026 noisy-label benchmark (7 datasets, 6 modalities, 10 LNL methods) finds current noisy-label techniques inadequate under real-world clinical noise, reinforcing that label construction quality is a hard constraint rather than a free regularizer.
- quote: "Experimental results demonstrate that current LNL methods are inadequate for effectively addressing label noise in medical imaging, particularly in high and real-world noise"
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09315v1
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026 (arXiv 2512.09315)
- confidence: medium

### [14] Label ROI is architecture-conditional (SpeedSci, 187th): v10 labels helped DINOv2 but v9 labels were better with ConvNeXt, so multi-source label blends should be A/B'd against the final backbone rather than assumed monotone.
- quote: "in my experiments, v10 labels + DINOv2 outperformed v9 labels + DINOv2 — but when I switched to ConvNeXt, v9 labels + ConvNeXt actually did better than v10 labels + ConvNeXt. It seems like different architectures have different 'ability' to benefit from label improvements."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: 2026-09 (comment ~23 days before 2026-09-29)
- confidence: medium
