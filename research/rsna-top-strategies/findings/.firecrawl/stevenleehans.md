4,407 training studies**, measured at **0.8873** against\n\nthe gold annotations — where the usual regex-derived labels score **0.8136**.\n\n* * *\n\n## Credit\n\nThis dataset exists because of public work by others. Named, with links, because that is\n\nwhere credit belongs.\n\n- **Pilkwang Kim** — [rsna-knee-baseline-v1](https://www.kaggle.com/code/pilkwang/rsna-knee-baseline-v1)\n- **Karnakbayev Artur** — [rsna-knee-eda-to-2-5d](https://www.kaggle.com/code/karnakbaevarthur/rsna-knee-eda-to-2-5d)\n- **Will / wguesdon** — [rsna-knee-dinov2-at-meniscus-resolution](https://www.kaggle.com/code/wguesdon/rsna-knee-dinov2-at-meniscus-resolution)\n- **Alexandre Moritz**, **Roman Rozen** — public EDA and baselines\n\nThe idea that report-derived labels are the binding constraint came from measuring their\n\npipelines, not from improving on them.\n\n* * *\n\n## The thing worth knowing before you train another model\n\n**Only 58 of 4,407 training studies carry real annotator labels** for the twelve findings.\n\nEveryone else builds targets by parsing the free-text radiology report — and the quality of\n\nthat parse sets a ceiling no encoder, resolution or ensemble can reach past.\n\nMeasured against those 58 gold studies:\n\n| label source | macro AUC vs gold |\n| --- | --- |\n| regex / lexicon extraction | 0.8136 |\n| **this dataset (v1)** | **0.8780** |\n| **this dataset (v2)** | **0.8873** |\n\nReports exist on **100% of train and 0% of test**, so text can only ever make _targets_ —\n\nnever a model input.\n\n* * *\n\n## Files\n\n- **`llm_labels_v4_blend.csv`** — recommended. Blend of v2 with [lixin73's GPT-5.6-Sol labels](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/lixin73/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels-sol56) (CC0). **0.8927** vs gold.\n- **`llm_labels_v2.csv`** — ours alone, 0.8873.\n- **`llm_labels_full.csv`** — v1, kept for reproducibility.\n\nBoth are one row per study: `StudyInstanceUID` plus the twelve findings as probabilities\n\nin `[0, 1]`.\n\n**`0.5` is not a coin flip — it means the report does not address that finding.** 25.4% of\n\nall cells in v1 are exactly 0.5. If you weight your loss by label confidence, `2·|p − 0.5|`\n\nis the natural weight and correctly sends those to zero.\n\n* * *\n\n## What changed in v2, and why it is interesting\n\n**Synovitis was the worst column in v1 at 0.678 AUC — and 83.7% of its cells were**\n**\"not addressed\".** Yet 27 of the 58 gold studies _have_ synovitis. Synovitis is common in\n\nthe joint and seldom written down; the labeller was being honest, and the honesty destroyed\n\nthe label.\n\nRadiologists report **effusion** readily, and the two co-occur (P(synovitis \\| effusion) =\n\n0.63, versus 0.22 without). So we tested one thing:\n\n```apache\nLLM Synovitis field vs gold Synovitis : 0.6780\nLLM Effusion  field vs gold Synovitis : 0.7115\ncontent_copy\n```\n\n**A field that is not about synovitis predicts synovitis better than the synovitis field**\n**does.** v2 fills only the undecided Synovitis cells from Effusion, never overriding an\n\nexplicit statement. Synovitis rises 0.678 → 0.790 and the key overall 0.8780 → 0.8873.\n\n* * *\n\n## Honest limits — please read these\n\n- **58 studies is a small ruler.** Differences below roughly 0.02 macro are not measurable\n\n\non it. Treat small gaps as unknown, not as zero.\n- **Gold prevalence is the annotator's sampling, not disease prevalence.** Every gold study\n\n\nhas at least one positive finding; mean 4.14 findings per study.\n- **A better key is not automatically a better model.** We swapped these labels in and got\n\n\nno gain on the first attempt; the gain arrived only after unrelated pipeline bugs were\n\n\nfixed. Validate on your own folds.\n- **These are model-generated labels, not ground truth.** They are a cheaper, better\n\n\napproximation of the report's content than a regex — nothing more.\n- **Per-label quality varies a lot**: ACL 0.993 and MCL 0.964 at the top, Synovitis 0.790\n\n\nand Fracture 0.793 at the bottom.\n\n* * *\n\n## Provenance\n\nGenerated by an LLM reading the `Report` column of `train.csv` from the\n\n[RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection)\n\ncompetition. No data outside that competition was used. Prompted for a probability per\n\nfinding with an explicit \"not addressed\" option, batched, and validated against the 58\n\ngold-annotated studies.\n\n* * *\n\n## v4: two labellers beat one, and the weaker one helped most\n\nBlending our key with **lixin73's** independent GPT-5.6-Sol labels:\n\n| key | macro vs gold | rank corr with ours |\n| --- | --- | --- |\n| ours v2 | 0.8873 | — |\n| Pilkwang | 0.8700 | 0.834 |\n| lixin73 | 0.8347 | 0.718 |\n| **v2 + lixin73 (v4)** | **0.8927** | — |\n\n**lixin73's key scores lowest alone and adds the most** — because it is the least\n\ncorrelated with ours. Standalone quality is not leg value; decorrelation is.\n\nBlend in **probability space, not rank space**. Percentile ranks are uniform by\n\nconstruction, so a `2*|p-0.5|` confidence weight computed on ranks becomes a function of\n\ncorpus position rather than of certainty.\n\nexpand\\_moreView more\n\n##
