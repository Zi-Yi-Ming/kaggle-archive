## About Dataset

# RSNA Knee: your labels are the bottleneck

LLM-scored report labels for **all 4,407 training studies**, measured at **0.8873** against

the gold annotations — where the usual regex-derived labels score **0.8136**.

* * *

## Credit

This dataset exists because of public work by others. Named, with links, because that is

where credit belongs.

- **Pilkwang Kim** — [rsna-knee-baseline-v1](https://www.kaggle.com/code/pilkwang/rsna-knee-baseline-v1)
- **Karnakbayev Artur** — [rsna-knee-eda-to-2-5d](https://www.kaggle.com/code/karnakbaevarthur/rsna-knee-eda-to-2-5d)
- **Will / wguesdon** — [rsna-knee-dinov2-at-meniscus-resolution](https://www.kaggle.com/code/wguesdon/rsna-knee-dinov2-at-meniscus-resolution)
- **Alexandre Moritz**, **Roman Rozen** — public EDA and baselines

The idea that report-derived labels are the binding constraint came from measuring their

pipelines, not from improving on them.

* * *

## The thing worth knowing before you train another model

**Only 58 of 4,407 training studies carry real annotator labels** for the twelve findings.

Everyone else builds targets by parsing the free-text radiology report — and the quality of

that parse sets a ceiling no encoder, resolution or ensemble can reach past.

Measured against those 58 gold studies:

| label source | macro AUC vs gold |
| --- | --- |
| regex / lexicon extraction | 0.8136 |
| **this dataset (v1)** | **0.8780** |
| **this dataset (v2)** | **0.8873** |

Reports exist on **100% of train and 0% of test**, so text can only ever make _targets_ —

never a model input.

* * *

## Files

- **`llm_labels_v4_blend.csv`** — recommended. Blend of v2 with [lixin73's GPT-5.6-Sol labels](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/lixin73/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels-sol56) (CC0). **0.8927** vs gold.
- **`llm_labels_v2.csv`** — ours alone, 0.8873.
- **`llm_labels_full.csv`** — v1, kept for reproducibility.

Both are one row per study: `StudyInstanceUID` plus the twelve findings as probabilities

in `[0, 1]`.

**`0.5` is not a coin flip — it means the report does not address that finding.** 25.4% of

all cells in v1 are exactly 0.5. If you weight your loss by label confidence, `2·|p − 0.5|`

is the natural weight and correctly sends those to zero.

* * *

## What changed in v2, and why it is interesting

**Synovitis was the worst column in v1 at 0.678 AUC — and 83.7% of its cells were**
**"not addressed".** Yet 27 of the 58 gold studies _have_ synovitis. Synovitis is common in

the joint and seldom written down; the labeller was being honest, and the honesty destroyed

the label.

Radiologists report **effusion** readily, and the two co-occur (P(synovitis \| effusion) =

0.63, versus 0.22 without). So we tested one thing:

```apache
LLM Synovitis field vs gold Synovitis : 0.6780
LLM Effusion  field vs gold Synovitis : 0.7115
content_copy
```

**A field that is not about synovitis predicts synovitis better than the synovitis field**
**does.** v2 fills only the undecided Synovitis cells from Effusion, never overriding an

explicit statement. Synovitis rises 0.678 → 0.790 and the key overall 0.8780 → 0.8873.

* * *

## Honest limits — please read these

- **58 studies is a small ruler.** Differences below roughly 0.02 macro are not measurable


on it. Treat small gaps as unknown, not as zero.
- **Gold prevalence is the annotator's sampling, not disease prevalence.** Every gold study


has at least one positive finding; mean 4.14 findings per study.
- **A better key is not automatically a better model.** We swapped these labels in and got


no gain on the first attempt; the gain arrived only after unrelated pipeline bugs were


fixed. Validate on your own folds.
- **These are model-generated labels, not ground truth.** They are a cheaper, better


approximation of the report's content than a regex — nothing more.
- **Per-label quality varies a lot**: ACL 0.993 and MCL 0.964 at the top, Synovitis 0.790


and Fracture 0.793 at the bottom.

* * *

## Provenance

Generated by an LLM reading the `Report` column of `train.csv` from the

[RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection)

competition. No data outside that competition was used. Prompted for a probability per

finding with an explicit "not addressed" option, batched, and validated against the 58

gold-annotated studies.

* * *

## v4: two labellers beat one, and the weaker one helped most

Blending our key with **lixin73's** independent GPT-5.6-Sol labels:

| key | macro vs gold | rank corr with ours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ours v2 | 0.8873 | — |
| Pilkwang | 0.8700 | 0.834 |
| lixin73 | 0.8347 | 0.718 |
| **v2 + lixin73 (v4)** | **0.8927** | — |

**lixin73's key scores lowest alone and adds the most** — because it is the least

correlated with ours. Standalone quality is not leg value; decorrelation is.

Blend in **probability space, not rank space**. Percentile ranks are uniform by

construction, so a `2*|p-0.5|` confidence weight computed on ranks becomes a function of

corpus position rather than of certainty.
