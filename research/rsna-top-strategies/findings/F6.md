# F6: Soft label failure modes

## Findings

### [1] CheXpert encodes report silence as a fourth class `blank` (no mention), distinct from negative `0`, uncertain `u`, and positive `1`; mapping missing/uncertain mentions onto negatives is explicitly warned to "distort the decision making of classifiers and degrade their performance."
- quote: "We assign (blank) if there is no mention of an observation. ... These approaches are similar to zero imputation strategies in statistics, and mimic approaches in multi-label classification methods where missing examples are used as negative labels. If the uncertainty label does convey semantically useful information to the classifier, then we expect that this approach can distort the decision making of classifiers and degrade their performance."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/1901.07031v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2019-01-21
- confidence: high

### [2] CheXpert's best uncertainty mapping is pathology-dependent, not a global rule: U-Ones (u→1) beats U-Zeros (u→0) on Atelectasis (AUROC 0.858 vs 0.811, p=0.03) and U-MultiClass beats U-Ignore on Cardiomegaly (0.854 vs 0.828, p<0.01), while Consolidation/Edema/Effusion show no significant gap.
- quote: "On Atelectasis, the U-Ones model (AUC=0.858) significantly outperforms (p=0.03) the U-Zeros model (AUC=0.811). On Cardiomegaly, we observe that the U-MultiClass model (AUC=0.854) performs significantly better (p<0.01) than the U-Ignore model (AUC=0.828)."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/1901.07031v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2019-01-21
- confidence: high

### [3] CheXpert U-Ignore (mask the loss on uncertain) is equivalent to listwise deletion and is biased unless uncertainty is missing-completely-at-random; for Consolidation the uncertain rate (12.78%) is nearly 2× the positive rate (6.78%), so a single global mask/map rule silently discards or mis-trains a huge slice of the label mass.
- quote: "Ignoring the uncertainty label is analogous to the listwise (complete case) deletion method for imputation ... Such methods can produce biased models if the cases are not missing completely at random. In this dataset, uncertainty labels are quite prevalent for some observations: for Consolidation, the uncertainty label is almost twice as as prevalent (12.78%) as the positive label (6.78%)."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/1901.07031v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2019-01-21
- confidence: high

### [4] MAPLEZ (LLM report labeler, Medical Image Analysis 2024) uses a five-way presence space — Present / Absent / Not mentioned / Uncertain / Stable — and explicitly refuses CheXpert's collapse of "Uncertain" and "Stable"; "Stable" is text-level comparison without presence, a distinct UNK that must not be taught as a probability.
- quote: "The five possible outputs for the categorical presence of an abnormality were \"Present\", \"Absent\", \"Not mentioned\", \"Uncertain\" ... or \"Stable\", i.e., the radiologist compares the state of an abnormality to the state from a previous report without indicating its presence or not (text uncertainty). We diverged from the CheXpert labeler's practice of assigning the same category to \"Uncertain\" and \"Stable\" cases, opting for distinct categories to allow for their separate handling."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04024v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-03-06 (v2 2024-08-15; MedIA DOI 10.1016/j.media.2024.103383)
- confidence: high

### [5] MAPLEZ ablation: forcing "Stable" UNKs into the presence loss as a 50% soft target ("Use Stable") hurts image-model AUROC versus simply ignoring them — Lung Opacity 0.919 vs 0.928, Pneumothorax 0.935 vs 0.940, weighted-all 0.918 vs 0.923 — i.e. a mid-value constant on silence/ambiguity is worse than dropping the term.
- quote: "'Use \"Stable\"' does not ignore the \"Stable\" abnormalities, setting them to a 50% probability ... we chose to ignore any abnormalities labeled as \"Stable\" for the presence loss."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04024v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-08-15
- confidence: high

### [6] MAPLEZ soft labels work only when they are calibrated presence probabilities used as BCE soft targets plus extra heads (location multi-task λ_loc=0.01): weighted AUROC 0.923 vs CheXpert 0.899 / CheXbert 0.912 / VQA 0.905 (all p<.001); dropping the probability channel ("Cat. Labels" only) falls to 0.919, and severity supervision adds nothing.
- quote: "The binary cross-entropy loss was employed in both cases, and the probabilities were used as soft labels. ... The supervision by the probability of presence and anatomical location provided small but statistically significant classification improvements in weighted area under the ROC curve (AUROC) (P<.001, P<.001). The severity annotations did not provide any classification improvements in the validation set."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04024v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-08-15
- confidence: high

### [7] Ushio, Ishida & Sugiyama (arXiv:2505.20761, ICLR 2026 poster) prove that even *perfectly calibrated* soft labels are not enough: corrupted / LLM-biased soft labels yield substantially inaccurate estimates, and calibration only repairs them if the corruption is order-preserving (isotonic calibration is then statistically consistent).
- quote: "we reveal that calibration guarantee is not enough, that is, even perfectly calibrated soft labels can result in a substantially inaccurate estimate. Then, we show that isotonic calibration can provide a statistically consistent estimator under an assumption weaker than that of the previous work."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20761v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-27
- confidence: high

### [8] Same paper names LLM annotator bias and labeling-distribution shift as a first-class soft-label corruption source, with CIFAR-10H (soft labels averaged over 47–63 human hard labels) as the worked example; over-confident soft labels (corruption a<1) under-estimate residual error and under-confident (a>1) over-estimate, and asymmetric b≠0.5 skews the whole vector.
- quote: "a similar issue can arise due to subjectivity of human soft labelers, or the bias of using large language models (LLMs) as annotators. ... the parameter a makes the soft labels over-confident when a<1, leading to an underestimation of the Bayes error, and under-confident when a>1, causing an overestimation. On the other hand, setting b to values other than 0.5 results in asymmetric, skewed corruption."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20761v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-27
- confidence: high

### [9] Müller, Kornblith & Hinton (NeurIPS 2019): soft targets (label smoothing toward uniform) improve calibration but *erase inter-class logit structure* — tight within-class clusters, lost resemblance information — which is exactly the signal a student or a second-stage image model needs, so training an image model on over-smoothed soft labels is a known failure mode, not a free win.
- quote: "if a teacher network is trained with label smoothing, knowledge distillation into a student network is much less effective. ... label smoothing encourages the representations of training examples from the same class to group in tight clusters. This results in loss of information in the logits about resemblances between instances of different classes, which is necessary for distillation."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02629
- source_type: primary
- published: 2019-06-06 (v3 2020-06-10)
- confidence: high

### [10] Lukasik et al. (ICML 2020): label smoothing is algebraically equivalent to injecting symmetric label noise and is only competitive with loss-correction when the underlying labels are already noisy; under noise-free conditions the same softening that helps calibration can actively hurt, and teacher-side smoothing helps only in the noisy-distillation regime.
- quote: "While label smoothing apparently amplifies this problem --- being equivalent to injecting symmetric noise to the labels --- we show how it relates to a general family of loss-correction techniques from the label noise literature. ... when distilling models from noisy data, label smoothing of the teacher is beneficial; this is in contrast to recent findings for noise-free problems."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02819
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-03-05
- confidence: high

### [11] DeBERTa-RAD (arXiv:2505.01693) shows soft/LLM labels *can* lift an image/report pipeline when certainty status is preserved as its own target and distilled with a tailored KD loss: LLM pseudo-labels including certainty → DeBERTa-Base student, Macro F1 0.9120 on MIMIC-500, with explicit strength on uncertain findings and on negation/uncertainty that rule-based labelers miss.
- quote: "We leverage an advanced LLM to generate high-quality pseudo-labels, including certainty statuses, for a large corpus of reports. Subsequently, a DeBERTa-Base model is trained on this pseudo-labeled data using a tailored knowledge distillation strategy. Evaluated on the expert-annotated MIMIC-500 benchmark, DeBERTa-RAD achieves a state-of-the-art Macro F1 score of 0.9120 ... Our analysis shows particular strength in handling uncertain findings."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01693
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-03
- confidence: medium

### [12] Cole et al. (CVPR 2021) and Zhang et al. (Hill loss / SPLC) frame the multi-label missing-label regime that silence-as-negative creates: unmentioned labels become false negatives, so losses must re-weight negatives (Hill) or constrain the expected number of positives (SPLC / single-positive variants) instead of treating every zero as a confirmed negative.
- quote: "When the number of potential labels is large, human annotators find it difficult to mention all applicable labels for each training image. ... As a result, multi-label training data is often plagued by false negatives. ... The first is a novel robust loss for negatives, namely the Hill loss, which re-weights negatives in the shape of a hill to alleviate the effect of false negatives."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09708
- source_type: primary
- published: 2021-06-17 (Cole, CVPR 2021); companion loss paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07368
- confidence: medium
