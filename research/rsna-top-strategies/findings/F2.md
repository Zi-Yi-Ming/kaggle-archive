# F2: Label engineering winners

## Findings

### [1] Labels are the binding constraint: only 58 of 4,407 train studies have expert labels; the other 4,349 are report-derived, and "every lever downstream tops out at whatever ceiling the label quality sets."
- quote: "58 of 4,407 training studies carry expert labels. The other 4,349 carry only the original radiology report. Deriving labels from that text is the actual competition, not a preprocessing step: every lever downstream (model architecture, augmentation, ensembling) tops out at whatever ceiling the label quality sets."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-09
- confidence: high

### [2] Public LLM report labels (stevenleehans v2) score 0.8873 macro-AUC vs the 58 gold studies, beating regex/lexicon extraction at 0.8136; the recommended v4_blend reaches 0.8927.
- quote: "LLM-scored report labels for all 4,407 training studies, measured at 0.8873 against the gold annotations — where the usual regex-derived labels score 0.8136. ... llm_labels_v4_blend.csv — recommended. Blend of v2 with lixin73's GPT-5.6-Sol labels (CC0). 0.8927 vs gold."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/stevenleehans/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (updated ~2026-07)
- confidence: high

### [3] Multi-source label merge improves gold AUC most when the second labeller is decorrelated, not when it is stronger: lixin73 alone is worst (0.8347) but adds the most to v2 (0.8873 → 0.8927); Pilkwang alone is 0.8700.
- quote: "lixin73's key scores lowest alone and adds the most — because it is the least correlated with ours. Standalone quality is not leg value; decorrelation is. Blend in probability space, not rank space."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/stevenleehans/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (updated ~2026-07)
- confidence: high

### [4] Soft-label convention: 0.5 means "report did not address the finding" (25.4% of v1 cells); loss weighting by 2*|p-0.5| correctly zeroes those cells.
- quote: "0.5 is not a coin flip — it means the report does not address that finding. 25.4% of all cells in v1 are exactly 0.5. If you weight your loss by label confidence, 2·|p − 0.5| is the natural weight and correctly sends those to zero."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/stevenleehans/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (updated ~2026-07)
- confidence: high

### [5] Cross-field label repair: Synovitis was the worst column (0.678 AUC, 83.7% "not addressed"); filling only undecided Synovitis cells from the Effusion field (P(synovitis|effusion)=0.63 vs 0.22 without) raised Synovitis 0.678→0.790 and overall 0.8780→0.8873.
- quote: "A field that is not about synovitis predicts synovitis better than the synovitis field does. v2 fills only the undecided Synovitis cells from Effusion, never overriding an explicit statement. Synovitis rises 0.678 → 0.790 and the key overall 0.8780 → 0.8873."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/stevenleehans/rsna-knee-llm-report-labels
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (updated ~2026-07)
- confidence: high

### [6] Rank-18 (top 2.3%) pipeline fused up to three label sources with per-class reliability weights measured on the 58 gold studies; disagreement becomes a reliability-weighted soft label rather than a majority hard vote.
- quote: "Each source's hard vote is weighted by its own measured per-class accuracy against the 58 gold studies (so a source that is unreliable on Synovitis specifically gets less say on Synovitis specifically, not blanket-penalized). When every source's hard vote agrees, the label is an exact 0 or 1. When they disagree, the output is a soft label: a reliability-weighted average of every vote."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-09
- confidence: high

### [7] Rank-18 prompt-engineered a local Qwen3.6-35B extractor against the 58 gold studies, iterating on error classes (Synovitis FN×14 broadened; Effusion FP×16 severity-graded; Contusion over-call sharpened to traumatic pattern): 0.828 → 0.833 mean accuracy, 0.766 mean F1, ~3 s/report.
- quote: "The prompt was not written once and trusted. It was validated against the 58 gold studies, scored, and revised. An early version reached 0.828 mean per-class accuracy against expert labels... The revised prompt reached 0.833 mean accuracy, 0.766 mean F1, at roughly 3 seconds per report."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-09
- confidence: high

### [8] Report-derived labels have an intrinsic ceiling of ~0.88–0.92 vs gold because gold labels come from expert image re-reads, not the text report; reports exist on 100% of train and 0% of test, so text can only make targets, never model inputs.
- quote: "gold labels here come from an expert re-reading the actual images, not from the text report, so even a perfect report reader has an intrinsic accuracy ceiling somewhere around 0.88 to 0.92, because a report and an image do not always agree. ... Reports exist on 100% of train and 0% of test, so text can only ever make targets — never a model input."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-09
- confidence: high

### [9] Severity-grading mild findings lower and sharpening soft labels away from 0.5 produced the largest single gains for a 0.74-OOF team: Effusion 0.628→0.726, ACL 0.775→0.855 (mild "geringer Gelenkerguss" scored as negative); "GOLD" 0.653→0.725 after sharpening because "BCE against targets clustered near 0.5 barely trains."
- quote: "Grading mild findings lower. I found reports saying \"geringer Gelenkerguss\" (slight effusion) annotated as negative, so scoring every mention at 0.85 was systematically wrong. Effusion went 0.628 → 0.726, ACL 0.775 → 0.855. Sharpening the soft labels: BCE against targets clustered near 0.5 barely trains. GOLD went 0.653 → 0.725."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/738339
- source_type: community
- published: unknown (~2026-08/09)
- confidence: medium

### [10] Community consensus: every jump from 0.87 → 0.93+ came from better (mostly LLM) label extraction, not architecture; one competitor hit 0.949 single-model by switching to Gemma 4 for local label extraction; "single prompt LLM extraction has not been beaten by more sophisticated extraction procedures."
- quote: "every jump from 0.87 → 0.93+ came from better label extraction (mostly LLM-based), not from architecture changes. One competitor got 0.949 single-model just by switching to Gemma 4 for local label extraction. The consensus emerging is that a \"single prompt LLM extraction has not been beaten by more sophisticated extraction procedures\" -and the image model (\"student\") consistently outperforms the label extractor (\"teacher\")."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/738339
- source_type: community
- published: unknown (~2026-09)
- confidence: medium

### [11] nartaa's second-pass label set (gemini-3-flash re-read with present/absent/not-addressed + calibrated probability + quote, silent findings keep prior) raised fold-0 gold58 epoch-ensemble AUC from 0.888 (v0raw/v4_blend) to 0.904 (gemlow and blend); 58-study noise is ~±0.03 so the +0.016 is at the edge of measurability.
- quote: "every report re-read by gemini-3-flash (reasoning effort low) against the host's image-annotation definitions... On fold 0 (870 held-out studies) with the same CoAtNet recipe, the epoch-ensemble score on the 58 gold studies was 0.888 with labels_v0raw, 0.904 with labels_v1_gemlow and 0.904 with labels_v1_blend. With 58 studies the noise is about ±0.03."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/nartaa/rsna-knee-hpo-assets
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (updated ~2026-09-25)
- confidence: high

### [12] Gold-override / holdout protocol used by top label pipelines: the 58 gold studies are fold −1 and never train; per-label gold AUC is too noisy to pick among label sets (nartaa perlabel pick "did not beat the blend"); a model trained on one label set always scores best on its own report truth, so only gold58 or LB can choose between label sets.
- quote: "folds_v0.json: folds 0–4 hold 869–870 studies each; the 58 gold studies are fold −1 and never train. ... A model trained on one label set always scores best on its own report truth, so these truths can't choose between label sets. Use the gold studies or the leaderboard for that."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/nartaa/rsna-knee-hpo-assets
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (updated ~2026-09-25)
- confidence: high

### [13] Swapping the extractor LLM is noise on 58 gold (qwen2.5:14b 0.8619, qwen3:14b 0.8572, qwen3:30b-a3b 0.8677; ~0.010 spread); reasoning mode collides with JSON-constrained decoding and silently eats answers (12/58 and 52/58 unparsed until thinking off).
- quote: "Swapping the LLM is worth nothing. On all 58 gold: qwen2.5:14b rev4 0.8619, qwen3:14b 0.8572, qwen3:30b-a3b 0.8677. A 0.010 spread on 58 studies is noise. Watch out for reasoning models - qwen3's block collides with JSON-constrained decoding and silently eats answers."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743148
- source_type: community
- published: unknown (~2026-09)
- confidence: medium

### [14] Extraction is near its ceiling: an audit of gold positives missed by report extraction found 207/240 caught and 22/33 misses were reports that never name the finding (synovitis, fracture, effusion); image→generated-report→labels is a lossy circular bottleneck and a BGE-M3 embedding probe scored 0.8423 vs 0.8843 for LLM+regex extraction.
- quote: "Extraction caught 207 of 240 positives, and 22 of the 33 misses were reports that never name the finding at all... Image -> generated report -> extracted labels is image -> labels with a lossy text bottleneck... a linear probe on BGE-M3 embeddings of the reports scored 0.8423 on 58 gold, against 0.8843 for LLM+regex extraction."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743148
- source_type: community
- published: unknown (~2026-09)
- confidence: medium

### [15] A high-end single-shot LLM+regex extractor reading 0.9083 label-AUC on the 58 gold still maps to only 0.935 single-fold LB, and teams report effnet-b0 0.94+ LB with only 0.88 extraction-label AUC via 5-fold + TTA — so past ~0.89 gold label AUC, inference/ensemble/TTA and unpublicized tricks (pseudo-labels) matter more than further label polish.
- quote: "LLM extracted labels got 0.9083 label auc on 58 gold and 0.935 lb on single fold... There are a number of teams who have reported taking effnet b0 to 0.94+ on LB with 5 fold ensemble + TTA with 0.88 AUC on extraction labels... there is probably a bit of headroom in modeling or unique tricks from that point that I dont think have gone public in detail like pseudo labels?"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743148
- source_type: community
- published: unknown (~2026-09)
- confidence: medium
