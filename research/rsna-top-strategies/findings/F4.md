# F4: Inference ensemble tricks

## Findings

### [1] Metric is unweighted mean of 12 per-label ROC AUCs, so ranking-only: top writeups skip threshold tuning and probability calibration, and treat plain sigmoid mean as a valid ensemble.
- quote: "Only ranking matters, not calibration: ROC AUC is invariant ... averaging model outputs is a safe way to ensemble: since the metric only cares about order, and sigmoid outputs from independently trained folds already sit on a comparable scale, a plain mean of several folds' probabilities is a defensible ensemble with no learned weighting required."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown (competition window 2026; LB pull 2026-08-09)
- confidence: high

### [2] Rank-18 public submission uses a plain 5-fold mean of sigmoid outputs at inference — no learned weighting, no calibration stage.
- quote: "loads all 5 fold checkpoints, and simply averages their sigmoid outputs, a plain 5-model ensemble with no learned weighting."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown (public LB 0.903, rank 18/792 as of 2026-08-09)
- confidence: high

### [3] Study-level score = mean of series-level logits after selecting up to 3 series (prefer fluid-sensitive, one per plane sagittal/coronal/axial) — this is the community's "multi-clip" pattern, not a learned plane weight.
- quote: "pick_series picks up to three: it prefers series flagged fluid-sensitive ... and tries to get one from each anatomical plane (sagittal, coronal, axial) ... A study's final prediction is the mean of its series-level logits."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [4] Eval-time slice sampling is deterministic even spacing (no jitter); train-time uses even spacing plus random jitter — same selection logic shared train/infer.
- quote: "at training time, evenly spaced positions with a small amount of random jitter added for augmentation; at evaluation time, exactly evenly spaced with no randomness, so the same study always gets scored the same way."
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [5] Code-comp runtime degradation ladder used in a real top-2% kernel: every 25 studies re-project runtime; first drop eval slices 32→16, then drop ensemble 5 models→3; write full submission every 25 studies; failed decodes get neutral 0.5.
- quote: "the kernel tracks its own projected total runtime every 25 studies and degrades in two stages ... first dropping evaluation slices per series from 32 down to 16, and if that is still not enough, dropping from 5 models in the ensemble down to 3. It also writes a complete, valid submission.csv every 25 studies"
- url: https://huggingface.co/blog/bishnoiyash/rsna-competetion
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown (public score 0.903, submission 55361257)
- confidence: high

### [6] TTA is a small, real win for a 9th-place single model at 288×288: 0.942 raw → 0.943 with TTA (+0.001); OOF pseudo-labels correlate with LB for this team.
- quote: "So far 0.942 raw, 0.943 with TTA at 288x288. OOF pseudo-labels have been pretty well correlated with LB."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-09-05 (posted 24 days before scrape 2026-09-29)
- confidence: high

### [7] Multi-fold ensemble is a modest lift over strong single fold for 3rd-place Scott Willis: single model single fold 0.938 → 5-fold ensemble 0.947 (+0.009).
- quote: "I'm at 0.938 with a single model, single fold. ... I did break down and submit a 5 fold ensemble though, so my current 0.947 is no longer a single fold."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-08-29 / update ~2026-09-04
- confidence: high

### [8] Cross-fitted folds + blend with a public stack is the documented multi-model path: CoAtNet-2@352 single fold 0.940 → 4 cross-fitted folds 0.942 → blend with public stack 0.945.
- quote: "My own model is a CoAtNet-2 at 352.. single fold 0.940, four cross-fitted folds 0.942.. and blended with the public stack 0.945."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-09-27 (posted 2 days before scrape)
- confidence: high

### [9] A 0.903-tier blend uses 4 recipes × 5 folds = 20 checkpoints: rank-average folds within recipe, rank-normalize family outputs, then equal-weight mean of 3 families per label (weights untuned); specialist replaces generalist on 4 columns.
- quote: "four model recipes, each trained on five folds: 20 checkpoints total. Fold predictions are rank-averaged within each recipe, and the resulting family outputs are rank-normalized for composition. Each label receives three equal-weight family contributions; I have not tuned those weights."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740610
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-09-29 (thread cites recent fold-count discussion)
- confidence: high

### [10] Concrete label-wise blend recipe after rank-norm: specialist-owned columns (ACL/MCL/menisci) = (specialist + CoAtNet + second DINO)/3; other eight columns = (generalist + CoAtNet + second DINO)/3.
- quote: "Four specialist-owned columns: (specialist + CoAtNet + second DINO) / 3 / Other eight columns: (generalist + CoAtNet + second DINO) / 3"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740610
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-09-29
- confidence: high

### [11] Multi-fold is not automatically better in this comp: ConvNeXt-T one-fold 0.939 (last-epoch) beat 5-fold ensemble 0.938 (best-val); Dread Development shipped single fold 0.941 in 23 min vs prior 5-fold 0.939 in 5 h.
- quote: "My Five fold ensemble models score:0.938(Pick highest valid auc) / One fold score:0.939(Pick last epoch)" and "My latest submission that I have been working is a single fold, 0.941 at 23 minutes / My prior was a 5 fold at 0.939 scoring in 5 hours"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740375
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-09-10
- confidence: high

### [12] Pipeline stages ranked as first-class levers for inference quality: series/plane selection, slice sampling, per-series/per-plane pooling, cross-plane aggregation; a stronger backbone still loses if these are wrong.
- quote: "series / plane selection ... anatomically meaningful slice sampling ... per-series / per-plane pooling ... This means that a better backbone can still lose to a weaker backbone if: slices are physically misordered ... irrelevant end slices dominate sampling"
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740164
- source_type: community
- published: unknown (2026 competition window)
- confidence: medium

### [13] Efficiency LB is evidence that many high scorers are not 20-model stacks; public notebooks over-ensemble for exposure, and complementary families (not more of the same) are the better blend direction.
- quote: "Highly doubt the top results are ensembles, look at the efficiency lb, you can see many high scoring submissions also have decent run times, i.e. they're not running 20 different models." and "the most promising direction to me feels like: good labels + a strong single model + a few truly complementary models blended together."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-08-29 / ~2026-09-20
- confidence: medium

### [14] "Single model" LB claims that use OOF/ensemble-derived pseudo-labels already embed ensemble knowledge (Deotte, 86th), so treat 0.93+ single-model claims as partially hidden ensembles.
- quote: "There are two types of single models. Those using original report extracted labels and those using pseudo labels. If we use pseudo labels, then every single model can match our best ensemble CV score and reporting that hides the fact that it isn't actually 'single model'."
- url: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304
- source_type: community
- published: ~2026-09-27
- confidence: high
